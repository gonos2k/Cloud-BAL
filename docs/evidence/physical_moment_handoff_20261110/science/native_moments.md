## PSD relations and conditions

KDM6's active PSD uses λ = (pidn Nvol/(DEN q))^(1/3); equivalently Nspec = q λ^3/pidn after dividing Nvol by the same DEN. Cloud uses generalized gamma shape muc=2 and `pidnc=cmc*Gamma(1+3/(muc+1))`; rain uses mur=1 and `pidnr=cmr*Gamma(1+3+mur)/Gamma(1+mur)`; ice uses mui=0 and `pidni=cmi*Gamma(1+3+mui)/Gamma(1+mui)`. `cmc=cmr=π denr/6`; `cmi=π*500/6` (3729–3732, 3778, 3808, 3836). Thus cloud and rain do not share a gamma factor. Choice of λ is a declared prior, not an observed number.

First local slope fallback treats QR ≤ 1e-9 or NRvol ≤ 1e-2 as inactive (4093); subsequent cap branch uses QR ≥ 1e-9 and NRvol ≥ 1e-2 (1868–1879). Cloud fallback uses QC ≤ qmin or NCvol ≤ ncmin, whereas cap branch uses ≥ (1853–1895); ncmin is selected from land/sea arguments (1091–1093). Ice branch uses QI ≥ 1e-14 and explicitly handles zero NI before applying the inclusive λ caps (1898–1914). Caps λ in m⁻¹ are cloud [1.2e4, 5e5], rain [961, 3.5e4], ice [9.08e3, 1.82e6]; cap comparisons use ≤ and ≥, recomputing number from mass. Threshold equality participates in both fallback and cap branches at different stages; an initializer should not infer one universal strict/inclusive rule.

`ProgB_param` (3937–4036) acts when QG > 1e-9 or BG > 1e-15, obtains QG/BG and clamps density to [100,900] kg m⁻³, then adjusts BG = QG/density. A missing BG with meaningful QG requires an explicit density prior; missing and zero observations cannot automatically mean clear sky. Negative zero has a special branch in the native code, so do not approximate that branch as a general missing-value policy.

At 2052 rain intercept is proportional to NRvol, and at 2224–2230 positive rain QR drives PREVP proportional to that intercept and RH−1. With zero NR, intercept and PREVP are zero even when rain and subsaturation exist. With positive NR and physical positive diffusional terms, PREVP is negative under subsaturation, subject to native mass and saturation bounds (2237–2244). Thus zero NR is not a neutral placeholder and subsaturated rain must be allowed to evaporate and cool; imposing RH=1 erases that behavior.

## Representative state contracts to validate

1. No observation, valid background: retain its water, temperature, and moments, including numbers; report provenance as background.
2. Warm saturated cloud: QC and NC must remain jointly meaningful; a prior for missing NC states a cloud PSD parameter and uses the cloud gamma law. Any water phase transfer must conserve total water and moist enthalpy with the existing phase-transfer/saturation routines.
3. Subsaturated rain: QR and NR specify a rain PSD; do not force saturation. Native PREVP can evaporate rain and cool. A zero NR with positive QR is observably different from positive NR.
4. Missing moments: for meaningful QC/QI/QR choose explicit species PSD prior only where no valid moment exists; for QG choose explicit particle density before deriving BG. Log prior and source, retain valid supplied moments, respect first-use constraints.
5. Mixed phase and melting: retain simultaneous liquid and ice/rain/graupel; phase transfers require water and enthalpy accounting, and QG/BG must remain physically bounded. Do not collapse mixed phases merely by temperature.
6. Observed clear versus unknown: explicit observed clear may support removal via a conserved phase-transfer policy; missing observation preserves valid background. An absent observation is not evidence of zero water.

These contracts call for end-to-end testing on the final interpolated/advection grid and separate validation of the existing `apply_water_phase_transfer`, enthalpy and saturation paths. They do not by themselves establish actual full-WRF runtime or forecast skill.
