# Low-threshold cohorts and capture protocol

This is a retrospective diagnostic of the existing, sealed six-hour RN1 evaluation, not a new admission rule or a training set. The fixed admission remains 21/26; PR154 is not adopted and B1 remains selected. Each threshold uses observation `< t` and forecast `>= t` for a false alarm, with equality at the threshold classified as an event. A **new** false alarm additionally has baseline forecast `< t`; a **resolved** one has baseline forecast `>= t` and PR154 forecast `< t`. All leads refer to the preceding hourly precipitation, not accumulation since initialization.

At +6 h, ORIGINAL to PR154 new false alarms at 0.1, 0.5 and 1 mm are 115, 138 and 167. Of these, 115, 136 and 160 respectively are outside the old 128 points. PR154 is below 5 mm at 115, 136 and 162 respectively. The union at 0.5 and 1 mm contains 233 distinct points, 226 outside the old cohort. For B1 to PR154, the 0.5 and 1 mm new false alarms total 110 and 134, with 106 and 125 outside. The old cohort is observation <5 and B1 <5, split by PR153 and PR154 at 5 mm into persistent 65, new 26 and resolved 37. `classification.csv` includes every one of these 128 points and every one of the 233 low-threshold union points, with per-threshold membership and all four forecasts. These are overlapping sets, not independent samples.

`lead_bins.csv` counts observed and forecast intensity bins at all six leads; `weak_detection.csv` separately shows, for each actual observed [0.1,0.5) and [0.5,1) bin, the number of forecasts below 0.1, between 0.1 inclusive and 1 exclusive, and at least 1 mm, plus detection at 0.1 and the number simultaneously between 0.1 and 1 for B1, PR153 and PR154. This distinguishes weak-rain detection from a maintained weak-intensity forecast.

## Capture selection

The 21 unique points in `selected_points.csv` are a limited process sample, not a statistical estimate of all transitions. Selection uses only the already evaluated observation, forecasts, latitude and longitude; it does not inspect future process captures. A deterministic L1 medoid is chosen in each stratum after independently range-scaling latitude, longitude, observation and the four forecasts within that stratum to [0,1]; constant dimensions contribute zero. The sum of distances to every point in the stratum is minimized, with the smallest flat RN1 index breaking ties. Already selected points are excluded. The protocol is:

1. Choose one medoid in each populated +6 h ORIGINAL-to-PR154 new false-alarm regime outside the old cohort: 0.5-only with PR154 in [0.5,1), 1-only and both thresholds, each with PR154 in [1,2.5) and [2.5,5). This gives five points.
2. Choose two +6 h 0.1-mm new-FA medoids outside the old cohort, one also a 0.5-mm new FA and one not.
3. For each of 0.1, 0.5 and 1 mm select a resolved false alarm closest to the first selected point qualifying as a new FA at the same threshold. Distance is equirectangular angular distance; smallest flat index breaks ties. Controls cannot duplicate prior selections.
4. Choose one medoid each for observations [0.1,0.5) and [0.5,1) where all three B1, PR153 and PR154 forecasts lie in [0.1,1).
5. Choose one medoid each from the old +6 h persistent, new and resolved groups.
6. Include the named +1 h false alarms 10813, 11571 and 11855 and their existing nearest retained-hit controls. These controls have observation and all three forecasts at least 10 mm, with ties resolved by flat index.

Each row includes the observation-grid flat ID, crop anchor, full WRF anchor, and bilinear weights. Full WRF zero-based anchors equal crop anchors plus (j=5,i=74); Fortran indices are one greater. The four corners are `(j0,i0)`, `(j0,i0+1)`, `(j0+1,i0)`, `(j0+1,i0+1)`, in both index conventions. Their weights sum to one and apply unchanged at all 39 layers. Native capture must retain all four corners and 39 layers before interpolation. The prior manifest, published before adding the weak-detection table, is preserved under `attempts/pre_weak_detection`; its selected CSV is byte-identical to the final manifest.
